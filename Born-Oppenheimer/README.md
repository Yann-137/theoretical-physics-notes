## Abstract
We consider a molecule composed of $N$ nuclei labeled by index $\alpha=(1..N)$ and $K$ electrons labeled by index $i=(1..K)$. Solving the Schrödinger equation for molecules is extremely challenging because of the sheer number of variables. Indeed, each electron and nucleus having a 3D position, the number of variables in the molecular wave function is astronomically large. Instead of solving Schrödinger’s equation simultaneously for all these variables, the Born-Oppenheimer approximation calculates the electronic wave function by considering the nuclear positions as fixed parameters. This is justified by the fact that nuclei are significantly heavier than electrons, and therefore nuclei move at very low speeds compared to electrons. In this approximation, the movement of nuclei is sufficiently slow to allow electrons to instantaneously adapt to changing nuclear configurations. Said differently, as nuclei move around the system remains in the same instantaneous electronic eigenstate without undergoing transitions to other states. This is the essence of the Adiabatic Theorem, which we will demonstrate in the first section of this paper. Once the wave functions for the fast-moving electrons are determined, the complete molecular state can be projected on the orthogonal basis of electronic energy eigenstates.
